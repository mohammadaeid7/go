## About This Program

The goal of this curriculum is to prepare students for entry-level Data Scientist roles using **100% free, open-source materials**. The program is:

- ✅ **Self-guided** and **self-accredited**
- ✅ **Collectively maintained** and open to contributions
- ✅ **Equivalent quality** to top paid programs
- ✅ **Project-based** with real-world applications

Upon completion, students can award themselves a [Credential](https://help.accredible.com/add-your-credential-to-linkedin) on LinkedIn from the Open Source Data Science Masters.

> 💡 **Pro tip**: This works best as a team sport! Try finding study partners on [r/learndatascience](https://www.reddit.com/r/learndatascience/).

## Program Structure

### 1. 📖 The Core
*Critical foundation - don't skip these!*

**What is Data Science?**
- [The Signal and The Noise](https://bookshop.org/a/2958/9780143125082) - Real-world Data Science cases
- [Dataclysm](https://bookshop.org/a/2958/9780385347396) - Human behavior through data
- [Informatics of the Oppressed](https://logicmag.io/care/informatics-of-the-oppressed/) - Ethics in algorithms

**Scientific Process & Problem Solving**
- [Problem-Solving Heuristics](https://math.berkeley.edu/~gmelvin/polya.pdf) - Pólya's "How To Solve It"
- [The Scientific Process](https://courses.lumenlearning.com/waymaker-psychology/chapter/reading-the-scientific-process-replace-content/)
- [Research Design Guide](https://www.scribbr.com/methodology/research-design/)

**Data Fundamentals**
- [SQL School](http://bit.ly/sqlschool) - Essential querying skills
- [Linear Algebra Guide](https://betterexplained.com/articles/linear-algebra-guide/) - Mathematical foundations
- [Stats in a Nutshell](https://bookshop.org/a/2958/9781449316822) - Statistical thinking

**Python Programming**
- [Learn Python the Hard Way](http://bit.ly/ebook-learnpyhardway) - Programming basics
- [pandas tutorials](http://bit.ly/py-pandas) - Data manipulation
- [Data Analysis in Python](http://bit.ly/mode-python-tutorials) - Applied skills

### 2. ❄️ Specialty: Choose 2
*Pick what interests you most*

- **🧠 Machine Learning**: [Stanford CS 229](http://bit.ly/stanfordcs229), [scikit-learn](http://bit.ly/scikit-video-tuts)
- **📝 Natural Language Processing**: [Stanford CS147](http://bit.ly/nlpcs124), [NLTK](http://bit.ly/ebook-nltk)
- **🌐 Graph Analysis**: [Social Networks](http://bit.ly/stanford-socialeconnetworks), [NetworkX](http://bit.ly/py-networkx)
- **📊 Visualization**: [Interactive Data Viz](http://bit.ly/interactive-data-viz-web)
- **🔗 Causation**: [The Book of Why](https://bookshop.org/a/2958/9781541698963)
- **🧮 Optimization**: [Convex Optimization](http://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf)

### 3. 🤝 Doing Data Science
*Professional skills and teamwork*

- **Communication**: [Craft of Writing Effectively](https://buomsoo-kim.github.io/learning/2020/03/30/Craft-of-writing-effectively.md/)
- **Business Impact**: [Technical Debt in ML](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/43146.pdf)
- **Industry Insights**: [Data Science Handbook](http://amzn.to/1J7lILJ)

### 4. 🧑‍💻 Capstone Project
*Demonstrate your skills*

1. **Choose** a meaningful dataset
2. **Formulate** a clear hypothesis  
3. **Document** your process in a spec
4. **Show** your work (preferably in Jupyter)
5. **Graduate** and share your achievement!

## Getting Started

1. **Start with the Core** - Build your foundation
2. **Join the community** - Find study partners
3. **Pick your specialties** - Choose 2 areas that interest you
4. **Practice continuously** - Work on real projects
5. **Share your journey** - Document and teach others
